# Changelog

All notable changes to Stalagmight are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/).
Update this file every time a new version is tagged.

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

#### Plan mode

- **The turn gauge reads a third of the gauge** - turn and drop pressures are now a share of the tank's gauge, as divers work them out by hand: a 12 L tank at 207 bar turns at 138 bar (141 before), one at 300 bar at 200 (211 before). Above about 96 bar a third of the gauge holds a little less gas than a third of the gas, so the turn is lower than the old gauge, and a diver who turns on it breathes somewhat more before turning (4.7% more at 207 bar, 13.5% at 300). Every tank still keeps at least two thirds of its real gas at the turn, and the reach drawn on the map only shrinks ([#576](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/576))
- **A moved stage drop decides the route** - the dive now goes through every stage drop you pin: past your way points, to each pinned drop in order, then on to your endpoint - doubling back where a drop is off its passage, with the extra swim counted - or, with no endpoint, as deep as it can go beyond the last drop. Before, a drop the planned route did not pass was ignored, so a change that moved the route (less gas, for one) gave back the gas the drop leaves behind and showed more reach than a diver who drops there has. When the gas runs out before a pinned drop, the dive turns on the way there, the stage is carried the whole dive, and the drop is shown grey as not reached. The reach colored on the map and the turn dots follow that dive too: only stations a diver who drops at the pinned stations can get to are lit, each priced after passing the drops, so a passage the dive does not go up is no longer shown in reach - even when the drop is pinned where its default already was. And when a pinned stage runs dry before its drop, the next stage starts at that drop rather than where the first ran dry; before, a smaller first stage could show more reach. A drop pinned where the dive cannot get to from the drop before it, in a part of the cave not connected to it, is ignored and that stage keeps its default drop; before, with no start station set, the map could go dark while still showing a turn and a route ([#576](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/576))
- **A scooter dive takes the way its range covers** - with a DPV, the plan now picks each route for the scooter range as well as the gas. Round a loop where the cheaper-gas way runs out of range, it takes the way round that the range covers, on gas you have, instead of turning short on range; stations on that way are lit and priced at what that way costs, and the range shown at the turn is what the route spends. Before, the range was counted only along the cheaper-gas way, so such a dive turned earlier than it had to, and the same dive with a stage drop pinned on the loop turned later than without it. This holds for every leg of the dive: the way to each way point and to each pinned drop takes the cheapest gas its range covers too, so a way point or a drop beyond a loop is no longer out of reach just because the cheaper-gas way to it is out of range. A pinned drop reached on a dearer way leaves a different share of its stage, so a smaller scooter range can change the budget of a plan with pinned drops - never above the same plan without them ([#576](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/576))

#### Ariane export

- **A junction whose depths disagree is exported at their average** - where a branch starts at a different depth than the station it hangs off, the `.tml`/`.tmlu` export now writes that station at the mean of its readings (the main line's, and one for each section hung there), keeps every other station at its true depth, and gives only the shots touching the junction a new length that keeps their horizontal run, so the plan stays put. Before, the branch was started at the main line's depth and every later shot on it was shifted by the whole mismatch. The export toast says how many junctions were averaged and the largest disagreement ([#576](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/576))

#### Saving on Windows

- **Writes to Windows paths that are not a plain file are refused** - on Windows, a save, export or other file write to a device or volume path (`\\?\GLOBALROOT\...`, `\\.\pipe\...`, `\??\...`), a path relative to a drive's current folder (`C:x.stalx`), an alternate data stream (`notes.txt:x.stalx`), a device name such as `NUL.stalx`, `COM1.stalx`, `LPT0.stalx`, `COM¹.stalx` or `CONOUT$.stalx`, or this computer reached as a network share (`\\localhost\C$\...`, `\\127.0.0.1\...`, also spelled `\\127.1\...`, `\\2130706433\...` or `\\0x7f000001\...`) is now refused with the reason, as a write into a system folder is; so is opening an older project at such a path, which would upgrade it in place. A network path spelled `\\?\unc\...` or `\\.\UNC\...`, and a folder name with a trailing dot (`C:\Windows.\`), now count as the folder they name ([#576](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/576))

### Fixed

- **Opening a project from an open one shows the loading window** - opening another project while one is open (Ctrl+O or a drop, then Discard in the unsaved-changes prompt) now shows the loading window over the project, as the project list does. While it is up, the Display menu and the keyboard shortcuts wait for it. A load that fails shows its error there with Retry and Dismiss; before, the error was not shown anywhere and the project's notices stayed hidden until the next load.
- **Display settings stay true after a stalled save of one** - when a display change (grid, grid spacing, geometry mode, a Display toggle) could not be saved in time before another project opened or the project closed, the screen kept showing it although it was never stored. The app now shows the stored setting again once the switch is done, and a later open or close no longer waits two seconds again for the stalled save.
- **"The project changed before this finished."** - a dive-plan save or delete, or a SpeleoDB action (start editing, done, push, publish, release lock, collaborators, project details, restore, unlink and the rest), that you started just before opening another project now says so in that short line, where it used to show its ordinary error text (or, for unlink, nothing). Background work (the SpeleoDB undo mode and web rendering switches) refused the same way stays quiet.
- **An edit made while a project opens says why it did nothing** - an edit, undo or redo made while another project is still opening (or the open one is closing) is refused, as before, but now a short note says "Wait for the project to finish opening." instead of nothing happening.
- **A cancelled load no longer ends in a "Loading error"** - pressing Cancel while a project opened could still bring up the loading error ("Operation cancelled", or "The file was replaced while it was being opened.") when the load's answer arrived after the Cancel. A load you cancelled now just stops.
- **A Cancel stops only the load it was pressed for** - Cancel on the loading screen or in an import dialog could reach the app after that load had already finished and a newer project open, new project or import had started, and cancel that one instead. Each Cancel now names the load it was made for, and a late Cancel no longer hides the progress of the newer load.
- **SpeleoDB:** projects Stalagmight publishes or adopts are now typed `stalagmight` on SpeleoDB instead of `other`, a type SpeleoDB never builds maps for. Projects already adopted as `other` are relabelled on their next push. Their maps update once SpeleoDB generates maps for Stalagmight projects.

#### Import

- **One import at a time, from the moment a file is picked** - a file opened, dropped or launched while an earlier import's file was still being read (no project open, or a slow or large file) was accepted, and the earlier file's late result then replaced the new file's dialog: with an error, or with a dialog for the earlier file. The new file now gets "Finish or cancel the current import first." while the first import carries on, and "Reading <file>..." shows at the bottom of the window while a file is read.
- **While an import's file is read, other files wait their turn** - a file opened with Stalagmight from the file manager while an earlier import's file is still being read now waits and opens once that import is done (finished, its error closed, or cancelled) instead of being turned away; Open / Import, Ctrl+O and Ctrl+I show "Finish or cancel the current import first." straight away instead of first opening the file picker; a recent project that is not a `.stalx` no longer has its error replaced by the earlier file's result; and "Reading <file>..." sits above the status bar instead of across its top edge.
- **A huge reading in an imported file no longer places stations at infinity** - a length, depth, elevation, passage dimension, length correction or angle that was finite but absurdly large (1e308) was accepted, and the computed station positions overflowed to infinity; a Walls rectangular vector that large passed the import preview and the import then failed. Walls, Compass, Ariane and GPX imports now read any such length beyond 10,000 km, or angle beyond 1,000,000 degrees, as missing and name it, with its line, shot or point, on the import preview and the receipt. As a backstop, an import whose station positions still come out non-finite is refused whole, naming the section.
- **A GPX track with a repeated point imports** - a GPS standing still logs the same point again, and each repeat became a zero-length shot that made the whole track fail to import. A point at the same position and elevation as the one before it is now skipped, and the import receipt counts them ("N repeated GPS points skipped"). A track or route whose points all repeat imports nothing, and the import preview and the receipt name it ("Track 'T' imports nothing: ...").
- **A negative Compass LRUD reads as missing** - Compass marks an unmeasured passage dimension with a negative number, but only `-1` and below were read that way, so a marker that went through a unit conversion (`-0.30`, -1 ft written in metres) imported as a negative passage dimension and was then flagged by validation. Any negative LRUD now reads as not measured, as in Walls.
- **An excluded shot no longer moves the depth of the shot after it** - with excluded shots left out of an Ariane import, a shot recorded as starting at the depth before it now starts at the depth the file gives the end of the excluded shot, as it already did when the excluded shot started its section. It used to take the depth of the shot before the excluded one, so the same survey could import a few centimetres differently in metres and in feet.

#### SpeleoDB

- **Convert to a local file no longer leaves the project in the SpeleoDB cache** - "Convert to a local file" (no-access prompt) and "Save my version as an unmanaged file" (divergence prompt) now ask where to save, starting in your projects folder with the project name filled in; Cancel changes nothing. The copy is written there unlinked, with its survey, settings and undo history, the cache copy is removed, the recent-projects entry and the open session follow the new path, and a held write lock is handed back. A destination inside the SpeleoDB cache is refused. Before, the link-less file stayed at the cache path and the next Open of the same project from SpeleoDB silently replaced it, local edits included. A link-less file found at a cache path (left by an earlier Unlink) now stops that Open with the "Changes on this computer are not on SpeleoDB" prompt: keeping the local copy asks where to save it and moves it out of the cache, taking the server copy discards it as before. Delete project on SpeleoDB (Admin tab) now treats the copy the same way: once the server delete has succeeded, it asks where to keep the local copy with the same Save As and moves it out of the cache, and the recent-projects entry and the open session follow it; Cancel leaves the copy unlinked in the cache, where that prompt protects it. ([#576](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/576))
- **Signing in over a live session releases the old session's lock** - signing in as another account, or on another server, while a session is live now does what logging out does for the old session: the lock it holds is released with the old token (the un-pushed-edits check still applies), edit mode is closed and the claim forgotten. Before, the old lock stayed held on the server and edit mode carried over to the new account. ([#576](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/576))
- **A lost reply to a lock request no longer leaves edit mode out of step** - when the reply to Release lock (project SpeleoDB settings), Done's release, Start Editing's lock acquire or Publish's lock acquire is lost, the app now re-reads who holds the lock at once. If the lock is gone or someone else holds it, edit mode closes (and Done finishes as released); if it is ours right after our own acquire, the claim is recorded: Start Editing opens edit mode and Publish's initial upload goes ahead, and a later quit or logout hands the lock back. If the re-read fails too (offline, server down), edit mode and the claim stay and the next successful status poll reconciles; after a Start Editing or Publish whose acquire went unanswered this way, the first status poll that finds your account holding the lock records the claim, so a quit hands the lock back, and a poll that finds the lock free or someone else's, or closing the project, drops the question. ([#576](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/576))
- **A write lock your account already holds is handed back like one you took** - when your account already holds a project's lock (from another Stalagmight window or computer, the SpeleoDB website, or this app before a restart), the app edits under it, and Done, closing, switching project, logging out, signing in as someone else, deleting the project and quitting now release it, as they release a lock taken here. Before, only a lock taken in the current session was released, so that lock stayed held until it was released from the held-lock prompt or the project's Admin tab. Viewing a revision while editing under such a lock is refused until the lock is released, as it already was for a lock taken here. ([#576](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/576))

### Security

- **Bundled SQLite updated from 3.45.0 to 3.54.1** - `.stalx` project files are SQLite databases you may be sent by others, and this picks up the upstream memory-safety fixes since January 2024, including several for crafted database files ([#598](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/598))
- **Bundled SQLite built without full-text search, R-tree, DBSTAT and extension loading** - Stalagmight uses none of them, and leaving them out means a crafted `.stalx` cannot reach their code ([#608](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/608))

## [0.5.0] - 2026-08-13

The first release with builds for all three platforms. The headline additions are
**Plan mode**, a gas- and range-aware dive planner built on the survey graph, and
**SpeleoDB integration**, which lets a project live on a server and be handed
between divers.

### Highlights

- **Plan mode** - plan a dive against the actual survey: gas-limited reach, a turn frontier, ordered way points, movable stage drops, DPV support, and a depth profile you can export ([#38](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/38))
- **SpeleoDB integration** - open a project from SpeleoDB, edit it under the server's single-writer lock, and push it back without leaving the app ([#182](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/182))
- **Export mode** - a what-you-see-is-what-you-get export surface over the map, sharing the survey map's coloring ([#239](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/239))
- **The data table is a spreadsheet** - an in-house grid with real copy/paste, draft rows, column families and per-column validation ([#61](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/61))
- **Loop closures are non-destructive** - a Display toggle between measured and closure-adjusted geometry; your raw shots are never rewritten
- **A custom WebGL map renderer** - large surveys hold far less memory and edits update the picture in place ([#80](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/80))
- **Walls import and Windows support** - `.wpj` / `.srv` surveys import, and the app builds and runs on Windows
- **Downloads for macOS and Windows** - releases now ship `.dmg`, `.msi` and `-setup.exe` alongside the Linux packages ([#324](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/324))
- **The map moves like a map** - drag to pan and scroll to zoom whatever tool you are holding, rather than switching to a Pan tool first ([#409](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/409))
- **Declination per section** - your measured bearings are stored exactly as recorded, and the correction to true north is applied only where the map or an export needs it ([#438](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/438))

### Added

#### Plan mode

- **Gas-limited reach** - Plan mode colors the survey by the gas it takes to reach each point and marks the *turn frontier*, the stations where the dive has to turn around. Set up back-gas and stage cylinders (any can be disabled without deleting the row), RMV and swim speed, then pick a **Gas strategy**: *All thirds*, *Half Minus: 1 stage*, *Half Minus: 2 stages*, or *Free*, where you set each cylinder's allowance yourself. A **safety margin** holds gas back from every cylinder. Every number shown - usable gas, turn pressure, drop pressures - comes from the same engine that draws the map, so a readout cannot drift from the reach it claims ([#38](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/38), [#228](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/228), [#245](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/245))
- **Routes you steer** - click a turn-frontier dot, or right-click anywhere, to set the endpoint and re-solve for it. **Way points** are stations the dive must visit in order, paid for by the whole solve rather than silently skipped; an unreachable one stalls the plan, because it is a constraint, not a hint. **Marked points** are free annotation. Every stage cylinder gets a drop marker you can drag or nudge: dropping deeper than the safe point is allowed but flagged red, dropping shallower honestly shortens the reach. Plan reports distance to the turnaround and the maximum and average depth along the route, with jump legs faded so a swum line reads differently from a shortcut ([#236](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/236), [#243](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/243), [#246](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/246), [#298](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/298))
- **DPV planning with per-segment speeds** - model a scooter rig (a primary plus a mandatory backup) in the **Equipment** tab. Way points divide the route into **segments**, and the **Segments** tab gives each one a speed and an on-DPV flag, with its own color on the route. A scootered span spends the rig's usable one-way range by the *battery clock*, so riding slower than cruise costs more range than line, and the panel says when it is range rather than gas that turns the dive. The park point is derived from the segments and marked on the map ([#317](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/317))
- **The planned dive as a profile** - a **Profile** tab charts depth across the round trip against distance or runtime, marking the turnaround, stage drops and way points. Every number is the one the gas engine already solved, so the chart cannot drift from the plan beside it. Exports as Subsurface-shaped dive XML or a fixed-width text table for a slate. Both carry a header saying what the file is not: Stalagmight has **no decompression model** - no ascent schedule, no stops, no tissue loading - and a profile must never be read as one ([#299](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/299))
- **Named dive plans persist** - saved in a sibling `.plnx` file next to the `.stalx`, never inside it, so a SpeleoDB push is untouched by it. Plans store stable station anchors, so they keep pointing at the same physical stations across later survey edits ([#244](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/244))
- **Survey, Plan and Export workspace modes** - three top-level modes from a carousel in the top bar. Plan and Export show the read-only closure-adjusted map and drop the mutating tools, so the survey cannot change under a plan or a preview ([#38](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/38))

#### SpeleoDB

- **Open, lock, edit and push** - a remote project is read-only until you take the write lock; local edits are fenced against the server tip, and when the two diverge a prompt offers keep-local, save-as-a-local-copy, or take-the-server-version behind a second confirmation. Lock holders are badged in the project lists, a lock left by a crashed session can be resumed or released, and revision history can be viewed and restored from. Remote projects you have access to appear in Global View, an Ariane-only remote project can be adopted as a native `.stalx`, and an opt-in force-edit lets you work offline or while someone else holds the lock. Your token lives in the OS keychain and never crosses into the web view ([#182](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/182))
- **Publish and manage projects in-app** - publish a local project (name, description, country, visibility, map location, collaborators), then manage details, per-user and per-team collaborators, lock release and deletion from a dedicated dialog with **General**, **Collaborators**, **History** and **Admin** tabs. Admin holds force-release of another user's lock, because that is stuck-lock recovery rather than part of editing. Links go straight to the same project on your server, and the toolbar's status mark right-clicks to the full menu ([#182](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/182))
- **Revert without taking the lock first** - the revision history offers **Revert** on a read-only project: it takes the lock, puts the revision back, uploads it as a new revision, and releases the lock, ending exactly as read-only as it started. History is never rewritten. Offered even when the local copy has changes the server has never seen, behind a two-step confirmation naming what it replaces - a state that previously offered nothing at all
- **A server dump with more than one openable file can be opened** - when a SpeleoDB project's dump holds several survey files, the app asks which one to use rather than dead-ending with an instruction to go tidy the server. The question is asked before the write lock is taken, the answer is remembered for the session, and it applies to opening a project as well as viewing and restoring a revision ([#258](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/258))
- **Credential storage you can see** - Settings reports whether your API key is in the OS keychain, a plaintext file, or held for the session, without interrogating the keyring (on Linux that is itself what raises the unlock prompt). If the keychain refuses the key at sign-in, it asks then and there whether to keep it for the session or write a file, rather than leaving you to discover later that you were signed out
- **One local file per SpeleoDB project** - a `.stalx` that belongs to a remote project now lives in exactly one place, the cache that Stalagmight pulls it into. Two local copies of the same remote project was a state where it was never clear which one your edits and pushes were going to, and it could be reached by ordinary use: Save As, or opening a copy someone sent you. Opening a linked file from anywhere else is now refused before the file is even read, and the refusal is a question rather than a dead end - open the managed copy instead, or cut this file's link to the server and keep it as a plain local project. Signed out or offline, cutting the link still works. **Save As on a project you have open from SpeleoDB is now "Save a Copy"**: it writes a `.stalx` with the server link removed and leaves your session on the managed file, so exporting a copy for someone can no longer quietly move which file you are editing - or which file gets pushed
- **Undo history that follows a SpeleoDB project through its history** - a linked project now stores the operations that produced each revision, so undo is no longer confined to one session. Undo to the start of your session, then use **Unwind to previous SpeleoDB version** at the bottom of the undo history to load the edit history stored with an earlier revision and keep going, revision by revision, back towards the creation of the file. Nothing is duplicated between revisions: each one carries only the operations made since the one before it. The SpeleoDB project dialog offers **No undo history** for very large projects, where undo still works locally but is stripped before every push ([#303](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/303))
- **The recent-projects list shows SpeleoDB write-lock state** as a colored bar down the leading edge of each card: you hold the lock, somebody else holds it, or it is free. Un-pushed local work still takes precedence in what the card reports, since that is the part that can actually be lost

#### Survey and data

- **Structural-integrity safety net** - Stalagmight now protects the relationships in a survey (station merges, loop closures, zero datums, comments). Edits that would corrupt them are refused before they take effect, and opening a project re-checks it; a banner offers **Review & Repair**, which lists what is wrong and repairs it in one click, dropping only invalid data. Cross-references are tied to each shot's stable identity, so inserting or deleting shots can no longer silently re-point a merge, closure, datum or comment onto the wrong station ([#17](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/17))
- **The data table is a spreadsheet** - shots and stations moved onto an in-house grid with a spreadsheet selection and keyboard model: copy/cut/paste, fill from a copied tile, column hide and reorder with position memory, a frozen gutter, per-column validation that flashes rejected input, and required cells that clear to a visible "missing" state rather than silently keeping or zeroing a value. Shots get draft rows for creating shots anywhere in a section, and azimuth/inclination **Avg** and **In+Out** column families that swap in place. Stations get coordinate groups, a zero-datum cell, and connection cells that open a merges/closures picker. A full-screen mode trades the map for room ([#61](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/61))
- **Warnings tab is a workflow** - **Resolve** / **Resolve all** with a live on-map ghost preview of each proposed fix, a virtualized list, double-click to pan, and per-warning fixes for azimuth, length, depth, LRUD and inclination-vs-depth-vs-length geometry. Resolved and ignored warnings persist across saves, keyed to the shot's stable identity. A **Survey Issues** color mode tints shots and stations green/yellow/red across merge groups. Validation thresholds are per-project, stored in the `.stalx` ([#22](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/22), [#57](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/57))
- **Jump and Reference are separate kinds of virtual shot** - a **Jump** is a shortcut a diver actually swims; a **Reference** is a survey tie nobody can swim, such as a tape between two mainlines. References are now excluded from penetration distance, the distance gradient, the ruler and gas planning, where they used to act as free shortcuts; jumps still count. Existing projects are classified once on open, and the import dialogs let you override per file ([#27](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/27))
- **Insert a station in the middle of a shot** - right-click a shot and choose **Insert Station...** to split it at a distance or percentage along its length into two collinear shots with a new station between them. Undoable and geometry-exact, and every merge, closure, datum and comment anchored past it keeps pointing at the same physical station ([#59](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/59))
- **Bulk shot deletion** - selecting a contiguous run of shots opens a **Delete Shots** dialog. An interior range asks whether to **split** the section in two or **close the gap**; a range touching either end closes the gap ([#95](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/95))
- **Toggle between measured and closure-adjusted geometry** - a *Geometry* switch in the **Display** menu. Applying loop closures no longer rewrites your measured data: the corrected geometry is computed on top of the measured survey and shown only while the toggle is on, so raw shots stay intact and adding closures re-solves cleanly instead of compounding error. The closure-adjusted view is read-only
- **Dual-distance ruler** - the measure tool reports both the **direct** 3D straight line and the **along-cave-line** distance following the surveyed passage. Endpoints snap to a station or an exact point on a shot. Endpoints in disconnected parts of the cave report no along-line distance rather than a wrong one ([#26](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/26))
- **Distance gradient overlay** - **Display -> Color -> Distance** colors the survey gold to purple by along-cave distance from the entrance, greys out anything unreachable, and shows a legend. Click a station or shot to re-root the gradient anywhere ([#34](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/34))
- **Declination is recorded per section, and applied only where it is needed.** The bearings you measured are always stored exactly as you recorded them; each section separately declares whether they were sighted against true or magnetic north and what the correction between the two is, and that correction is applied only when the map or an export needs true north. Compass and Walls imports keep the original readings and record the correction on the section rather than baking it into the numbers, and an Ariane import looks the historical declination up for you when the file carries a date, saying on the receipt when it could not. Exporting a survey whose sections disagree, to a format that has only one north, rewrites every bearing to true north when each section's correction is on record; when one is not, the readings go out exactly as measured and the export says so rather than guessing ([#438](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/438))
- **A zero datum can sit below the surface** - the zero-datum dialog gains a "Depth below surface" field, so a datum measured under water no longer has to pretend it sits at the water line ([#524](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/524))

#### Import, export and platforms

- **Walls (`.wpj` / `.srv`) survey import** - imports David McKenzie's Walls format from the Import menu, drag-and-drop, or <kbd>Ctrl+I</kbd>. A `.wpj` project tree, or a standalone `.srv`, is parsed into linked, GPS-positioned sections with merges, loop closures and one GPS anchor per connected part of the cave. The dialog offers per-survey selection, a splay toggle, comments-on-stations, duplicate-file detection and live counts
- **Export mode** - a large what-you-see-is-what-you-get preview over the map, driven by the same controls as the export dialog, rendering the closure-adjusted geometry the live map shows. A **Color** dropdown matches the survey map (PNG and PDF; SVG stays monochrome), and a **Comments & Labels** section mirrors the Display tab ([#239](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/239), [#240](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/240))

- **The first import into an empty project now offers to place the parts of the survey that float free**, and says so when it has nothing to suggest. A first import previously got no placement suggestions at all, even from a file that plainly contained both anchored and disconnected pieces ([#446](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/446))
- **The Ariane import dialog is a wizard now** - importing a `.tml`/`.tmlu` shows a map preview of the file's sections, per-section include toggles, and a placement step - the origin, the file's own coordinates, or coordinates you type - instead of silently placing the survey at the origin. The map pane's width can be dragged, is shared with the mNemo wizard, and is remembered ([#425](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/425), [#404](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/404))

### Changed

- **The survey map draws through a custom WebGL renderer** - both the single-system map and Global View render on a purpose-built renderer instead of MapLibre's stock layers. Very large surveys hold far less memory, hit-testing runs off the geometry index, and edits, drags and undo update the picture in place instead of re-tiling it. There is no fallback: if WebGL2 is unavailable the app tells you which part failed rather than quietly drawing something else ([#80](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/80))
- **In-app chrome reworked** - the Global View header and in-project toolbar collapse into one hamburger **App menu** built from shared rows. Panels become a single dockable instance with hover and pin modes, sizes and reveal modes persisted. The Display menu is rebuilt as element-colored pill categories, Open and Import route through one path, and a failed save offers a one-click retry ([#49](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/49), [#53](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/53), [#54](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/54))
- **The top bar condenses instead of overflowing** - labels drop in a fixed priority order as space runs out, then padding tightens, and everything returns when the space does
- **Loop-closure adjustment is faster on large surveys** - the least-squares solve collapses degree-2 chains before solving, turning a per-component O(n^2) into O(n) ([#81](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/81))
- **Loop-closure adjustment is now non-destructive** - the old "Adjust Closures" action that baked the correction into your shots is replaced by the measured/closure-adjusted toggle. Existing projects open unchanged; geometry a previous build already baked cannot be un-baked, but is never rewritten again
- **DMP placement suggestions rank better** - reranked using survey timing and cave-traversal constraints, with tighter thresholds and crossing rejection. The wizard's default is now the highest-confidence feasible suggestion, so accepting it no longer wires up the wrong connection ([#96](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/96), [#101](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/101)). The connection wizard also opens near-fullscreen instead of in a cramped modal ([#21](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/21))
- **SpeleoDB says "write lock", not "lock"** - so it reads as exclusive write access rather than the file being shut. The status mark shows a green pen while you hold it, not a padlock, which reads as "shut" to anyone who has not learned that holding the lock is the permissive state. The token-storage setting is gone: where the key is kept is decided when the keychain actually fails, and reported in Settings
- **Adopting an Ariane project now finishes the job** - adoption takes the SpeleoDB write lock, converts the `.tml`, uploads the resulting `.stalx` with its Ariane sidecar, and hands the lock back, so the converted project lives on the server instead of sitting in a local cache waiting to be pushed. Taking the lock *before* the download also closes the window in which another writer's push could make the tree being converted stale. If the lock cannot be taken - somebody else is editing, you have read-only access, the machine is offline - the project is still adopted locally, marked as having un-pushed edits, and the app says so; the SpeleoDB project `type` is only switched to "other" once the `.stalx` has actually landed, so a failed upload leaves a project that still offers Adopt rather than one nothing can open
- **A released build reports its release version** - a build of a tagged commit shows the bare version, since the tag already pins the commit; development and untagged CI builds keep a commit suffix, because there the commit is the only thing identifying them. The commit is shown on its own line either way
- **Persisted undo/redo history is cleared on first open of a pre-upgrade project** - entries saved by earlier builds speak the old reference flag and the old inclination sign convention; replaying one would silently undo those conversions
- **Toolbar no longer has Draw and Delete tool buttons** - deleting still works: select an element and press <kbd>Delete</kbd>, or use the right-click menu
- **The in-app guide is a first-launch orientation, not a walkthrough** - it is rewritten for someone who already knows how to survey: teach what is specific to Stalagmight, then get out of the way. It leads with the two things a surveyor cannot infer from a survey background - the **Survey / Plan / Export** workspace modes, and that only Survey can edit, with the mutating tools *hidden* rather than greyed out elsewhere; and the **measured / closure-adjusted** geometry split, which is read-only and which Plan and Export select wherever the project has closures to solve. Narration of the self-evident and the per-field DMP hand-holding are gone, taking 9 flows and 51 steps down to 7 flows and 12 steps. The orientation tour runs once, and only once a project is open for it to point at, so a first launch that never opens anything does not spend it. The contextual guides for DMP import and station placement are cut to a step each but still fire the first time you reach those workflows; they keep their flow ids, so an install that has already dismissed one will not be shown the shorter version. **Existing installs will see the new orientation tour once after updating**; **Settings -> General -> Restart onboarding** re-arms it, and every contextual guide with it, at any time ([#331](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/331))
- **Licensed under AGPL-3.0-or-later** - with contribution guidelines ([CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)) and a security policy ([SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)), in preparation for a public release
- The pinned toolchain moved to **Node.js 24** (`.nvmrc` plus advisory `engines`), so builds and tests now run on the current LTS line - relevant if you build from source. `scripts/mac-setup.sh` and `scripts/mac-build.sh` read the major from `.nvmrc` instead of hardcoding it, so they can no longer drift behind a bump. **pnpm moved to `11.18.0`** in the same way, via `packageManager`, now carrying a corepack integrity hash so a tampered pnpm tarball is refused rather than run; `scripts/mac-setup.sh` reads that field and installs the exact pinned version instead of a floating major. Build-script approvals move out of `package.json` into a new `pnpm-workspace.yaml`, which is where pnpm 10 and later read them. `pnpm-lock.yaml` is unchanged ([#109](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/109))
- CI: GitHub Actions bumped to versions that run on Node.js 24, ahead of the Node.js 20 runtime deprecation ([#351](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/351))
- `scripts/release.sh` now bumps `src-tauri/Cargo.lock` alongside the other three version files, so a release no longer leaves the lock a version behind. It rewrites only this crate's stanza, refuses to start if the lock is already out of step (including under `--dry-run`), tolerates a CRLF checkout, and reverts the version files if `$EDITOR` exits non-zero instead of stranding a half-finished release. `scripts/test-release.sh` covers all of it and now runs in CI, which it never did before - that is why the drift went unnoticed for three releases ([#352](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/352))
- The agent harness moved to Node 24 with everything else, so there is no second toolchain. Its "Node 22 only" rule blamed a webdriverio undici-versus-built-in-fetch mismatch that the code does not actually do; session creation works identically on Node 22 and 24, and `pnpm agent:http-probe` now checks that in one command after a webdriverio or Node bump
- Undo history for a SpeleoDB project is no longer capped at a fixed number of steps; it is bounded by total size instead, so ordinary editing keeps hundreds of steps while whole-survey operations (deleting a section, an import) keep as many as fit ([#303](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/303))
- Taking the write lock on a project that matches the server clears the undo history from the previous editor's session, so Ctrl+Z can never replay somebody else's operation. Locking a copy that still holds your own un-pushed edits keeps your history ([#303](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/303))
- **Draw Shot now stores the bearing you enter, and offers a "Reverse shot direction" checkbox instead of translating frames silently.** **Add Shot Before** used to prefill the copied bearing turned by 180 degrees and turn whatever you typed by another 180 before storing it. That was a frame translation, not a plain bug: the number on screen meant "the bearing you would sight standing at the existing station", the number stored was the new leg's own direction, and accepting the prefill stored the correct geometry - so shots entered that way are unaffected. What it never did was say which frame it wanted, and the data table's own insert-above-first-shot already stored what you typed verbatim, so the two producers disagreed by 180 degrees for a hand-typed prepend. Draw Shot now matches the data table: what you enter is stored as-is, in every mode, in the direction the section reads. **If you typed a bearing by hand into Add Shot Before in an earlier version, it was interpreted in the other frame - worth re-checking those legs.** To enter a leg you measured from its far end, tick the new checkbox under the azimuth fields: it turns the whole shot around - the two bearings swap and reciprocate, the depths swap ends, pitch negates and swaps, and left/right trade places. Off by default. Note that unlike **Reverse Section**, which asks before mirroring LRUD, this always swaps left and right
- **"Import" now means one thing, and it depends on whether a project is open.** The welcome screen's menu had both **Open / Import** and a separate **Import** entry that opened the very same file picker - the second one is gone, and Open / Import still accepts every file the app understands. Inside a project, **Import** is the button for adding mNemo data to what you already have, so it offers `.dmp` files only. That is the only format that can be added to an open project: choosing an Ariane, Compass, Walls or GPX file there could only ever offer to close your project and start a new one, which is what **Open / Import** in the menu is for. Ctrl+I follows the same rule, and dragging a file onto the window still accepts anything ([#508](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/508))
- **Satellite view is on by default.** New projects open with the imagery layer showing; turn it off from the Display menu and the choice is remembered per project as before
- **The `[cache.providers.<name>] offline_caching` config key has been removed.** `tile_cache_enabled` is now the only switch that turns tile caching on or off; per-provider entries carry only a `ttl_days` retention override. If you had set `offline_caching = false` the key is now ignored rather than honoured, so set `tile_cache_enabled = false` instead if you want caching off. Whether any individual tile is stored is no longer a config question at all - it is decided per response by the provider's own `Cache-Control` (see [docs/configuration.md](docs/configuration.md))
- **Satellite tiles are now cached, following the imagery provider's own caching policy.** A tile is stored only when the provider's response says it may be and for how long - Esri allows a day - and once that is up the app asks whether the tile has changed rather than downloading it again, so revisiting a site costs almost no data. Nothing is stored for a provider that grants nothing. A stored tile is never shown as current past its allowed time; it is only used as a last resort if the provider cannot be reached at all. Cache size and how long a tile is kept are configurable (see [docs/configuration.md](docs/configuration.md))
- **The map moves like a map now: drag it to pan, scroll to zoom, whichever tool you are holding.** Panning used to be a tool you had to switch to, so a left-drag did nothing at all under eight of the ten tools unless you knew about Ctrl+drag. A plain left-click-drag now pans from anywhere, and zooming is the wheel, a pinch, a double-click or the +/- keys, none of it tied to a tool any more. Only two things still claim the drag for themselves: the Zoom tool's box and placing a station. That includes while a move tool is carrying something: the drag pans, the picked-up geometry stays under your cursor and the map slides beneath it, because a move is dropped by a click that stays put - not by letting go of a drag wherever you happen to have got to. Ctrl+drag and middle-drag pan as well, and they alone are scaled by **Pan speed**, since dragging the map is now always 1:1 with your cursor. With nothing left of their own to do, the **Pan** and **Zoom** tools are off the toolbar by default and **P** and **Z** do nothing at all until you turn on **Settings -> Map -> Controls -> Show navigation tools**, which restores both buttons and both shortcuts - they are kept for anyone who cannot drag or scroll. Drag-box selection is removed with no replacement: it owned exactly the gesture panning needed, and Shift+click and Ctrl+click still build a multi-selection. On a tablet, one finger pans under every tool, and a two-finger twist zooms without rotating the survey, which a top-down plan was never meant to do ([#409](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/409))
- **Plan mode asks where the dive starts before it shows you anything else.** The opening question is "Where does this dive start?", with a one-click **Start from entrance** and your gas assumptions summarised underneath; the reach shading, the gas legend and the equipment panel appear once a start point is set. Geometry beyond your gas range now fades instead of being marked with red end-point dots, so out-of-range passage reads differently from passage that is simply not connected, and the turnaround is marked with a U-turn chip rather than a purple dot ([#395](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/395))
- **Every table you edit shots in is the same spreadsheet now.** The new-section dialog, Edit Section, the mNemo import wizard and Zero Datum setup all use the main data table's grid and column menu, so paste from a spreadsheet, fill-down, range selection, keyboard navigation and resizable or hidden columns work everywhere. Several long-standing faults went with the duplicates: a comma decimal typed into a number field silently became 0; editing a shot's average azimuth overwrote both legs with it instead of keeping their spread; an azimuth typed on a newly added shot could save as due north; a right-click on the map could flash its menu and close it again; deleting the last rows of a section could be refused; and Edit Section's warning column now actually shows warnings
- **Every failure now reports itself once, on the surface that suits it.** An autosave that failed used to leave no trace at all and now raises a banner with a **Save now** button that clears when a save lands; a backend error with no dialog of its own - dragging a station, using a shortcut - now says what went wrong instead of nothing. Import, save and SpeleoDB push failures share one wording and one surface, rather than a mix that sometimes doubled up and sometimes disagreed ([#415](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/415))
- **The two geometry views are renamed to Raw Data View and Closures Computed View**, from Measured and Closure-adjusted, and both carry an open or closed padlock so it is clear at a glance which one you can edit in ([#408](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/408))
- **Every remaining dropdown uses the app's own control** rather than the operating system's, so they all look alike and open in the right place inside a scrolling dialog
- **Opening a project from the recents list goes through the same checks as opening a file from anywhere else**, and accepts project files only, so a stale or hand-edited entry pointing at something else can no longer be opened by mistake. A message that could print your full file path, home folder and username included, for certain unsupported files no longer does ([#450](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/450))
- **The import wizards use their space better** - the map preview fills its pane instead of holding a centred square, the divider between the panes can be moved from the keyboard, the suggestion step's section settings fold away until needed, and the placement buttons stay visible on a short window ([#567](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/567))
- **Map drawing does less work per frame** - the WebGL layers look up their shader handles once when a program links instead of on every frame ([#339](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/339))

### Removed

- **Ariane collaboration mode** - the opt-in per-project mode that kept a sibling `.tml` next to the `.stalx`, saved both on every save, and reconciled external Ariane edits back in on the next open has been removed entirely. Plain Ariane `.tml`/`.tmlu` import and export are unaffected. `.stalx` schema v15 drops the now-unused `collab_state` table. This is one of the reasons persisted undo/redo history is cleared on first open of a pre-upgrade project (see Changed): a saved entry can name a collaboration operation this build no longer understands, and replaying the entries around it would corrupt the survey

### Fixed

#### Packaging

- **Every IPC call silently fell back to a slower transport for the rest of the session** - the production build's security policy did not list Tauri's own IPC origin, so the very first call to the backend failed its check and the app dropped to a slower fallback path for every call after. The policy now allows it, and two directives that had been left open with no safe default are now explicitly locked down as well

- **macOS: the `.app` in the DMG could not be opened at all**, with macOS reporting it as "damaged". The download was intact; the bundle was never signed. Without a signing identity Tauri skips code signing entirely, leaving only the Rust linker's ad-hoc signature on the inner binary, with no sealed resources and no `_CodeSignature` - a state macOS rejects as *invalid* rather than merely unsigned, and an invalid signature is refused outright. The app is now ad-hoc signed before the DMG is built, and the release workflow verifies the app inside the DMG, since that is the file people download. The build is still not notarized, so first launch is still blocked and has to be allowed once (see the Installing notes on the release) ([#350](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/350))

#### Survey correctness

- **Inclination from a mNemo is no longer stored upside down** - the device reports inclination positive-up while depths are positive-down, and DMP import kept the device's sign, so every non-horizontal DMP shot carried an inclination that contradicted its own depth change. Import now converts once, so a descending shot reads as a descent everywhere. **Existing projects are converted on open**, worked out from each section's own geometry, touching only sections where the evidence is one-sided; a shot whose inclination genuinely disagrees with its depths stays flagged. Dismissed inclination warnings are re-raised, since the number they were dismissed against has changed meaning. **Inclination shown for DMP-sourced shots changes sign**: a shot descending at 17 degrees now reads `+16.9` where it read `-16.9`. Walls and Compass shots are unaffected ([#296](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/296))
- **Exporting to Ariane no longer reverses a shot below a branch junction** - where a branch met the main line at a different depth, the shot hanging off that junction was written as starting where its parent ended while its own depth was left untouched, so a measured 0.5 m descent could come out as a 4.3 m ascent. Affects **Export to Ariane** and the copy uploaded to SpeleoDB; your `.stalx` was never wrong
- **Non-finite (NaN/infinity) geometry can no longer corrupt a survey** - rejected or neutralised at every entry point rather than propagating through merged sections
- **Deleting a shot on a loop no longer fabricates a false closure** - the closure is converted to a plain station merge instead of being kept as a bogus loop that shifts the geometry ([#8](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/8), related [#41](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/41))
- **Drifted station merges are detected and repairable** - merged stations could silently drift apart (one real project had a station 30 m from its counterpart) with no warning and no in-app fix
- **Two zero datums in one merged group no longer tear the merge apart silently** - a merge-connected group can hold at most one GPS anchor; a project that already has the problem now raises a warning on load
- **Merged sections move as a rigid group**, **merging across an unaligned loop closure is no longer wrongly blocked**, **merging projects keeps per-station comments**, and **shot identity survives undo/redo and project merges**
- **Importing into a brand-new project no longer loses the import** - a DMP imported into a project whose first save was deferred was never written to disk, so quitting lost the whole import ([#92](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/92), [#110](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/110))
- **A loaded plan's route points survive survey edits** - deleting or inserting stations could silently rebind a plan's origin, endpoint, way points and stage drops to whichever station slid into the same position. A point whose station is gone now flags itself and stalls the plan instead of quietly retargeting ([#310](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/310))
- **Undoing a station move keeps every shot field**, and **corrupt persisted undo entries no longer empty the whole stack**
- **Merging projects dropped their entrance elevation.** A merged file always exported an entrance elevation of 0.0 regardless of what the source projects held, because the merge never carried `surface_elevation` forward. The first source project with a value now wins, and a later source that disagrees beyond the existing 0.005 m tolerance produces a warning instead of being silently discarded ([#448](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/448))
- **Deleting a shot that split a section could weld two unrelated parts of the survey together and call the join a merge.** The two parts were independently anchored, so the file that came out of it would later report merged stations tens of meters apart. A stale cache let this past the check meant to catch it. **Repair** in the project menu now finds this specific damage in a file that already has it and puts the loop closure back
- **Deleting a shot or a loop closure could leave the survey slightly out of line** where a loop had been turned into a rigid connection. That conversion now always closes the gap it creates instead of sometimes leaving a small offset behind ([#464](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/464))
- **Two undo faults found by stress testing.** Undoing certain edits near a converted loop closure could reclassify it or leave closure data behind, and removing the origin of a station merge could leave a stale state until the file was reopened. Both are handled as they happen now ([#413](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/413))
- **Removing the station a survey's coordinates were anchored to silently moved everything** - map, data table and any export - by however far that anchor had shifted things. Every station keeps its real position now; only which station counts as home changes. The same fix covers adding the very first anchor to a survey made of several disconnected pieces, which could jump one piece relative to the others ([#469](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/469))
- **Warnings you had already dismissed could come back on the next save** of a project made by an older version, because the file's own record of which one-time upgrades it had been through could be wrong. A save no longer takes the file's word for it ([#485](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/485))
- **A save could rebuild the file with your list of ignored warnings erased, and say nothing about it.** Any difficulty reading that list counted as "there is no list" - including a lock briefly held by something else, an unexpected value in a single cell, or an older file shape - so the warnings you had dismissed came back and the dismissals were gone from the file for good. A problem in one row can no longer erase anything: an unreadable cell is coerced or trimmed, and a row that names no shot is dropped and counted. A problem with the table itself now refuses the save outright, before anything is written, and says so instead of sending you to a repair screen that cannot help. The list you see in the app and the list a save carries are read the same way now, so they can no longer disagree ([#517](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/517))
- **Project depth statistics reported a made-up range** - the min/max depth in the project-statistics IPC payload was a per-section walk of depth *changes* seeded at zero: a survey that never leaves 30-35 m came through as 0-5 m, and two sections at different depths cancelled instead of combining. No screen displayed these two numbers (the panels compute their range from station depths directly), but any consumer of the payload would have disagreed with every depth shown in the app. The payload now carries the real station depth range ([#481](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/481))
- **An unreadable edit history is bounded, discarded and reported in a banner**, instead of failing silently ([#473](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/473))
- **A survey can no longer outgrow its shot identity space** - a shot's identity is derived from its section's number, which caps how many sections a project can hold (42,949). Nothing enforced the cap: a large enough import, merge, split or plain section creation silently wrapped identities so shots in different sections collided. Every path that creates sections now refuses past the cap before changing anything, naming the limit, and opening a `.stalx` that already carries section numbers past it - written by another tool, or corrupted - is refused with a message naming the offending id, instead of corrupting shot identity ([#555](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/555), [#557](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/557))

#### SpeleoDB

- **Your own landed work no longer reads as a conflict** - pushing while keeping the write lock, editing again, then pushing raised "resolve conflict" against a commit you had just made yourself. Related routes into the same false conflict are closed: an upload whose reply carried no commit id, a publish whose first upload did not confirm, and a stale status reply resurrecting the "not uploaded yet" warning
- **Write locks behave when resumed, not just when taken** - resuming a lock you already held looked identical to taking it fresh, which could explain another session's upload away as your own and hide a real conflict. "Continue editing" on a stranded lock handed that lock back whenever the server had moved on, leaving un-pushed work in a project anyone could claim. Releasing the lock from Project Settings released it on the server but left the app believing it could still edit
- **Revert is safer** - it now stops and asks you to save or discard unsaved edits rather than replacing them anyway; a failed upload retries just the upload instead of redoing the whole revert (which discarded anything edited since, and could collide with its own just-uploaded revert); the Retry is reachable inside the dialog; and adopting an Ariane project re-checks what the open project owes immediately before replacing anything
- **The API key is handled honestly** - turning the OS keyring off now deletes the key it already held instead of leaving it on the machine; "keep for this session only" says so when a copy could not be removed rather than promising it will be gone; the storage prompt names where the key actually went when a file write fails; signing in with Settings open no longer has an unrelated save post a stale email back over it; and "Try again" no longer forgets that you declined the keychain. **You will be asked to sign in once after this update** - Stalagmight now records whether it used the keychain, and no previous version wrote that down. Nothing is lost
- **Leaving a project with un-uploaded work always says so** - opening another project, or starting a new one, only checked for unsaved-to-disk changes; opening a project from SpeleoDB checked nothing at all. Also fixed: a 401 mid-session hiding the failed-upload notice behind the force-edit banner, push being offered while force-editing when the server can only refuse, and removing a collaborator not asking first
- **A write lock can no longer be stranded by what happens while it is being taken** - taking the lock talks to the server, and closing, switching, deleting or signing out of the project during that exchange was not accounted for: the lock could come back and be recorded against a project that no longer existed, leaving a lock held on the server that nothing in the app could see or release. Taking a lock now checks that it is still the same open session it started in, and refuses with a retry rather than half-completing. The reverse case is closed too - deleting a project stood down its own pending release when a new session had already reclaimed the project - and the one state the old cleanup could never reach, a lock left behind with no project open, is now released when the app next checks the link
- **Deleting a project is safer about what it tears down** - deleting the open project cleared one field and left a live database handle on a file that was about to be removed, plus the whole undo history, so an undo could replay against a deleted project (and on Windows the removal failed outright while that handle was alive). The project is now properly closed first. A delete whose path cannot be read - a folder whose permissions you have lost - is refused instead of being treated as "already gone", which had been pruning the recents entry and handing back the server lock on a project that was still sitting there, un-pushed edits and all. Deletion also no longer follows a symlink to the file it points at, and a failure to save the recents list afterwards is reported rather than only logged
- **Restoring a revision no longer leaves a dive plan pointing at the wrong cave** - checking out a different revision swaps the survey in place, but an open gas plan kept its pins, its loaded-plan baseline and its station anchors. Because a pin can be positional, it silently came to name whatever station now sits at that index, and saving the plan afterwards could bake the restored tree's stations into the `.plnx`. This matters most where two `.stalx` live under one SpeleoDB project: they are independent databases with independently assigned section ids, so the ids collide by construction and the staleness guard - which compares pin values, not project identity - could not see it. The plan is now cleared on a restore, exactly as it is when you open a different project ([#345](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/345))
- **Adopting an Ariane project showed an empty screen** - adoption left the converted project open in the backend, so the app's own load of it was refused as "already open" and short-circuited to a view switch. The survey never appeared: the adoption had worked, and reported success, over a blank window. Adoption now hands the project back for the app to open normally
- **SpeleoDB map previews that never appeared** - the `ariane.tml` pushed alongside a commit is converted to GeoJSON server-side, and several shapes our exporter could emit made that conversion fail or be skipped without any error reaching the app (the upload still succeeded). The pushed sidecar is now hardened for it: shot lengths are written so they still cover the vertical drop after rounding, a survey that would emit a single shot is padded, dates are always valid ISO within the declination model's year range, `UUID` elements are emitted only when they are unique v4s, a GPS anchor on the equator or the prime meridian is nudged off zero, and the section-identity separator is stripped from section and surveyor names. The user-facing "Export to Ariane" output is unchanged
- **Adopting a project could report the wrong outcome** - three cases where adoption described itself inaccurately. It flipped the SpeleoDB project type a second time after the upload had already done it, so a transient failure on that redundant request warned that the type could not be changed when it had been. If the upload succeeded but handing the write lock back failed, it reported the conversion as "on this machine only" - about a survey that was already safely on the server - and marked the project as having unsaved work; that case now says the upload landed and that the write lock is the thing left outstanding. And taking the lock is now serialised against sign-out - the span covers the account read, the holder probe and the acquire together - so signing out during an adoption can no longer leave the project locked on the server with nothing recording it.
- **Adoption now tells you about everything it left unfinished.** If the connection dropped after the upload landed, the write lock and the project type could BOTH be left outstanding, but the notice mentioned only the lock - so after releasing it the project was still typed as an Ariane one, still offering to be adopted over a copy that already existed. Alongside that: a project whose type was changed to something else while the adoption ran was reported as re-typed with the server left untouched; a lock release whose reply was lost in transit is now re-checked against the server rather than reported as still held; and the proof that an upload landed now confirms it is reading the adopted project's own record.
- **Opening a survey from SpeleoDB could quietly drop shots, and the warnings that explained why.** Adopting a remote Ariane file threw away every conversion warning and never mentioned shots the conversion had excluded, so a file that excluded all of them could open as an empty survey with nothing said about it. Opening from SpeleoDB reports what a manual import of the same file would ([#411](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/411))

#### Legibility

- **Depth coloring actually shows depth again** - every theme's depth gradient now runs cyan through green and yellow to red, shallower to deeper. Some themes had drifted to ramps with almost no color travel across the whole depth range: the Cave theme ran tan to salmon, about 35 degrees of hue, so an entire survey drew in one near-uniform color and the legend's shallow and deep ends were hard to tell apart. Others took the gradient the wrong way round the color wheel - six of the eight had their shallow anchor placed just past the point where the shortest path between the two ends flips, so the ramp ran through blue and violet instead of down through green and yellow. Every shallow anchor now sits on the correct side of that line. The ramps also carry a lightness change alongside the hue change, so they stay readable with red-green color blindness, and each one is checked along its whole length rather than just at its two ends. The two light-canvas themes (the light theme and E-Ink Kaleido) are held to a slightly lower contrast floor than the rest on purpose, so the ramp stays vivid instead of being forced dark enough to read as olive and brown ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **The measure tool is visible on light mode** - its default cyan sat at 1.7:1 against the paper canvas, and resetting Display settings did not help because the light defaults specified the same cyan ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **Panel tabs, buttons and the scale bar are readable on every theme** - the secondary text color failed the contrast floor on six of the ten themes, which is what made the side-panel tabs and the Data button hard to read; the scale bar's border cleared it on none of them, including the default dark theme; and the scale-bar text was dark-on-dark for Solarized Light and Catppuccin Latte, whose map canvas is dark even though their panels are light ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **Section colors seeded for the canvas they will be drawn on** - a survey imported under a light theme was given the dark-tuned pastel palette, drawing at roughly 1.5:1 on the paper canvas from the moment it was created. New sections now seed from a palette chosen by the map canvas. Colors already saved in a project are never rewritten, so an existing survey keeps exactly the colors it had ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **Satellite imagery was displayed without its attribution.** Wherever Esri imagery is drawn - the survey map with Satellite view on, Global View, and the section preview - the map now shows the imagery's source names, as Esri's terms require. On a narrow map, and always in the small section preview, they collapse to an **i** button that shows them on hover, click or keyboard focus. The names are read from Esri's own service so they stay current, falling back to a built-in list when you are offline; the previous built-in list was out of date. The separate "Powered by Esri" credit, which Esri allows to live behind a button rather than on the map, is now in **Settings -> About** alongside a **Map imagery** section listing the sources
- **The coordinate grid was drawn on top of the survey**, hiding shots and stations underneath it whenever it was switched on. It is drawn beneath the survey, its ghosts and its loop closures now ([#461](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/461))

#### Settings and configuration

- **A crash or a full disk while saving settings can no longer wipe them** - `config.toml` was written in place, truncating the old file before the new contents landed, so an interruption left it empty or half-written. Nothing reported that: the load path treats an unreadable config as "no config", so every setting you had ever changed came back at its default with no error. Settings are now written to a temporary file and moved into place once complete, so a reader sees the whole old file or the whole new one ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **Two settings changes at once no longer overwrite each other** - the commands that persist panel sizes, the data-table layout and the reveal-in/out behaviour each read the whole configuration, changed their own part and wrote it all back, with nothing serialising the three steps. Two of them overlapping meant the second silently reverted the first, on disk as well as on screen, and it did not take contrived timing: releasing a panel divider saves immediately while a column resize saves 500 ms after you stop, and both live in the same view. Plan mode's gas fields had the same problem from the other direction - they posted the entire configuration built from a snapshot that could already be stale, so nudging a gas value could revert a column width you had just set. Gas settings now write only the gas section, and the rest is serialised ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **Settings > Advanced > Reset no longer clears your data-table layout** - it reset the whole panels section, which also holds column order, hidden columns, column widths and the azimuth/inclination display modes, none of which that page shows or lists among the changes it is about to make. The result was incoherent rather than merely broad: the open table did not notice, so the reset looked like it had done nothing until the next restart, and the next unrelated change to the table wrote the old layout straight back. Reset now touches only the three panel sizes the page actually offers ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **Advanced settings no longer accept panel sizes the app will not keep** - the page accepted a sections-panel width up to 800, a detail-panel width up to 800 and a data-table height up to 600, while the values actually enforced were 400, 500 and 500. Anything above the real limit validated, saved, was quietly clamped and came back changed, with the on-screen message still quoting the wrong range. The page now validates against the real bounds and states them ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **A data-table layout that fails to save now says so, and is no longer dropped on the way out** - a failed write was reported only to the developer console, so resizing a column and quitting looked identical to success. Closing the table or the project within the half-second save delay also discarded the pending change outright ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **`config.toml` stops growing on its own** - a stored column width was never removed once its column stopped existing, and the whole set was rewritten on every later resize, so obsolete entries were preserved indefinitely. Widths for columns the table no longer has are now dropped; a hidden column keeps its width, since it comes back ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **Settings saved through the app are now repaired on the way in**, as they already were on the way out, so a value that could not be rendered no longer sits in `config.toml` until the next launch quietly fixes it. Clearing a keyboard shortcut is still respected: an empty shortcut means you removed it, not that it is missing ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **An interrupted settings save could leave a stray file behind forever** - if the app crashed or lost power in the instant between writing a settings update and putting it in place, the in-progress copy was never cleaned up, and nothing ever would. Settings loading now sweeps away any such leftovers, and the in-progress copy is written in a way that can no longer be redirected by a file planted in advance at its name ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))
- **Settings -> Shortcuts pretended Pan and Zoom still worked.** With the navigation tools hidden (the default), their P and Z rows looked like any other shortcut but pressed keys did nothing, and giving either key to another action was refused over a conflict with a tool that has no button. The rows now show as disabled with a note pointing at the setting that brings them back; their keys stay reserved so turning the tools back on restores everything untouched

#### Interface

- **The help tooltips could not be reached from the keyboard**, and once a click had pinned one open there was no dependable way to shut it. The trigger is a proper focus stop now: Tab to it and the help appears, Tab away or press Escape and it goes - without also closing the dialog you were working in. A click pins it and a second click unpins it, a pinned tooltip closes on Escape or a click elsewhere, and a screen reader announces it once instead of reading the whole paragraph twice ([#532](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/532))
- **The SpeleoDB mark is visible on light themes** - the logo is white line art, and it was drawn as-is wherever it was not being deliberately tinted, so it disappeared into light-coloured chrome: in the project menu, on the status badge and on the lock badge. It already affected E-Ink Kaleido and the new light theme widened it. The mark now takes its colour from the text around it ([#357](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/357))

- **The grabbed point stays under the cursor while moving** - grabbing a segment, station or shot a few pixels off its attachment made the whole thing jump, worst when zoomed out ([#20](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/20), [#79](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/79))
- **Tool state no longer carries across a project switch** - the active tool and its pending targets survived loading another project, and because ids are small integers they resolve in the new project too, so the next click could act on the wrong station
- **Dropdowns no longer dismiss themselves as they open** ([#268](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/268)), **data-table sub-header dropdowns stay clickable when they flip upward** ([#162](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/162)), and **a toast can no longer swallow a click meant for a dialog**
- **Typing into a just-opened dialog no longer loses keystrokes** - the initial-focus heuristic ran a frame after opening and could yank focus from a control you had already clicked ([#289](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/289))
- **Section Adjust accepts negative and partial offsets** - the dx/dy fields rejected a leading minus sign, making it impossible to nudge a section in the -X or -Y direction ([#161](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/161))
- **Merging projects to a path with no extension no longer overwrites a file silently** - `.stalx` was appended after the "output already exists" check ([#169](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/169))
- **Anchored sections dim correctly under the move tools** ([#301](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/301)), and **shot and comment visibility are restored when a project loads**
- **macOS: a save blocked by revoked folder access explains itself** instead of reporting a misleading "corrupted database" failure ([#39](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/39)), and **scrolling over the map no longer drags the window** ([#43](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/43))
- **Light mode is actually light** - the theme switched the panels and controls but left the map canvas on the dark palette, so the app still read as dark. Light mode now themes the survey canvas too: station outlines, labels and the placement crosshair invert to dark ink, while the text halos and the grid invert the other way, against the paper. Section colors are per-project data and keep the palette they were saved with, so a survey saved in the default palette still draws those sections in their original colors - that palette was tuned for the dark canvas and reads faint on the paper one, so **new** sections created under a light canvas now seed from a palette tuned for it instead (existing ones are never rewritten). The two remaining light palettes, Solarized Light and Catppuccin Latte, keep the dark canvas for now ([#332](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/332))
- **Depth coloring follows the theme** - the depth gradient was pinned to the dark theme's colors for every theme, so in Depth display mode a light canvas drew its survey lines and station labels in colors picked for a dark background, at contrast low enough to make shallow passages invisible. Each theme's own depth colors are now used, and the on-map depth legend shows the gradient actually drawn rather than the dark one. E-Ink Kaleido had already declared its own depth colors and they were being ignored ([#332](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/332))
- **Data-table column widths survive a restart** - resizing a column in the shots or stations table is now remembered, alongside the column order and visibility that already persisted. Widths are stored per app, not per project, in `config.toml`, and a width that could not be rendered (a collapsed 0-pixel column, or one wide enough to push its neighbours off-screen) is repaired on load. The recent-projects card/list choice named in the same issue was already persisting and is unchanged; note it lives in the webview's `localStorage`, not `config.toml`, so unlike the column layout it does not travel with a copied config ([#334](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/334))
- **A guide step that cannot find what it points at no longer locks the app** - if a tour step's target never appeared, the overlay held a full-window dim and swallowed every click while its own card stayed invisible. Escape was not inert, but the confirmation it raised rendered inside that same hidden card, so there was no way out short of quitting. A step that fails to anchor within 1.2 seconds now centres itself, keeping **Next** and **Skip** reachable, which retires the dead-end for every step rather than the one path that reached it ([#331](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/331))
- **The closure solve announces itself to screen readers** - the "Computing Closures" message was injected together with the live region meant to speak it, the case assistive technology announces unreliably, so the only signal that a large project's solve was running could pass silently ([#333](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/333))
- **Depth-mode image export follows the theme too** - the exported PNG and PDF drew the dark theme's depth gradient whatever theme you were working in, so the file stopped matching the screen the moment each theme got its own depth colors. It read worst on the light theme, whose export page is white: the file came out in the dark theme's colors, at contrast low enough to be unreadable against white. The export now uses the same endpoints the map is drawing, and the WYSIWYG preview in the export dialog uses them as well, so preview and file cannot disagree ([#360](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/360))
- **The active tool's map cursor stops being erased** - the tool-to-cursor table existed in three copies that had drifted apart, two of them still naming tools removed long ago and disagreeing about the merge and move tools, so which cursor you got depended on which copy wrote last. Ending a Ctrl+drag pan under Move Station could also leave the four-way move cursor sitting over empty map, implying something was draggable there ([#316](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/316))
- **The map's color overlay could get stuck on Distance** - depth coloring is a saved preference while the distance overlay is not, so loading a project or restoring a SpeleoDB revision while distance was on wrote the saved depth setting back in underneath it. With both set, the overlay read as Distance, and picking Depth - or turning coloring off entirely - silently did nothing. Selecting a color mode now always lands on the one you picked, and loading a project no longer leaves the previous project's distance overlay, or the station it was measuring from, behind ([#34](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/34))
- **Turning map coloring off now sticks** - if you switched to Distance or issue-severity coloring while depth coloring was your saved preference and then chose None, depth quietly came back on the next launch. Whether None sticks no longer depends on the route you took to it
- **Reopening the data table could undo a column resize** - closing the table right after resizing a column and reopening it before the save had finished landing could resurrect the previous widths and write them back over the new ones
- **Resizing or hiding a data-table column could clear an active ruler measurement** - with the closure-adjusted geometry view on, the data table's own save was discarding cached map geometry it had no reason to touch, which cleared an in-progress ruler measurement and made the map briefly drop back to un-adjusted geometry while it recomputed
- **The data table could fail to open against an older backend** - if the saved settings predated the column-width and column-order fields, opening the data table threw instead of falling back to defaults
- **Letting go of a map drag over a button or panel edge left the map convinced it was still moving.** Releasing a drag on top of the floating chrome that overlays the map - the +/- zoom buttons, the scale bar, a panel handle, the Data tab - never reached the map's drag handler, which kept waiting for a release it had already missed until the next gesture shook it loose. The release is now delivered no matter where the cursor lets go, in Single System, Global View and the section preview alike
- **Ctrl+click never added anything to a selection, or took anything out of one.** The multi-select toggle the app has always documented - Shift+click to add, Ctrl+click to toggle - could not be reached with a mouse: the Ctrl+drag pan took the press the instant the button went down, under every tool but Pan, and swallowed the click that came after it, so a Ctrl+click on a station or a shot did nothing whatsoever. A Ctrl press now only becomes a pan once it actually travels - the same three pixels at which the map itself stops calling a press a click - so the toggle works again and Ctrl+drag still pans, including while carrying a move. **On macOS the toggle is Cmd+click**, because macOS treats Ctrl+click as a secondary click and opens the context menu instead; Shift+click adds to a selection on every platform
- **A long-press on the map also tapped whatever was underneath it.** Holding a finger on a station or shot opened its context menu, and then lifting the finger ran the active tool's click through the map behind the open menu - with a mutating tool selected, that was an edit nobody asked for. The lift that ends a long press is now swallowed. Global View's long-press menu had the identical bug and is fixed with it. A long press also no longer opens the menu at all in the two moments a hold is already spoken for - while placing a station or carrying a move - where it used to both make the edit and cover it with a menu ([#338](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/338))
- **The toolbar could get stuck squeezed after hiding the navigation tools.** In a narrow window the toolbar drops button labels to fit; turning **Show navigation tools** off then freed up space it never took back, leaving icon-only buttons until the window was resized. It now re-expands the moment the setting changes either way
- **On a tablet, a two-finger pinch that landed on a map legend or button zoomed the whole app.** The pinch scaled the entire window like a web page instead of being ignored, and had to be pinched back out. The app window no longer page-zooms; pinch-to-zoom on the map itself is unaffected
- **Panning and zooming are noticeably smoother**, zooming especially: a large amount of per-frame work that the map did not need is gone ([#430](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/430))
- **A long load or save could appear to freeze partway.** Progress updates were being discarded as out of date when they were nothing of the kind, leaving the indicator stuck where it stopped ([#486](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/486))
- **Plan mode's Start from entrance could show an entirely unreachable survey with no explanation** when the project had no usable zero datum. It now says so, and points at setting one or picking a different start station ([#480](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/480))
- **A two-finger pinch on Global View zooms without twisting the plan** ([#338](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/338))
- **<kbd>Ctrl+=</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl+-</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl+0</kbd> are enforced as reserved shortcuts**, not just displayed as such, and the sections-row tooltip says a double-click zooms ([#319](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/319))

- **Cancelling the export file picker no longer counts as completing the export dialog** - the dialog stays open with your options intact, ready to try again ([#560](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/560))

#### Import

- **Backing out of the mNemo wizard duplicated everything it had already brought in, and cancelling kept it silently.** Back now offers to withdraw what the wizard imported, as one undoable step, and carries your settings - GPS, name, metadata, shot edits and the anchor station you picked - into the reopened dialog, so going back is adjusting one field rather than starting again. Cancel counts what has landed and asks: remove it and close, keep it and close, or carry on editing. A project created for an import is also no longer written to disk before the import commits, so an abandoned wizard does not leave a survey behind that was never imported ([#394](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/394))
- **Going back from the zero datum dialog lost what you had just typed.** What you entered is carried back and restored when you return to the same section; picking a different section starts fresh, and the confirmation says exactly that rather than promising to discard work it no longer discards ([#534](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/534))
- **The zero datum dialog explained neither what it was for nor what skipping it cost.** Skip sat beside the section cards as an equal choice, with nothing to say that a survey without a zero datum has no real-world position; it is now a secondary action that names its own cost, and the dialog defines what a zero datum is rather than assuming you know. Coordinates that look like a swapped latitude and longitude raise a gentle warning instead of being taken at face value, the GPS field shows what a valid entry looks like instead of leaving Confirm mutely disabled, and every way out - Escape, the backdrop, the close button, Cancel and Back - asks before discarding edits, across all eight editable fields rather than the three it used to notice ([#495](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/495))
- **The Walls, Compass and GPX import dialogs looked frozen while they checked for duplicates.** The Import button waits for that check, and nothing said so; it now reads "Checking for duplicates..." while the check is outstanding ([#495](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/495))
- **The import receipt left out two things it already knew**: shots kept in place because their section is anchored at both ends, which were counted only in the log, and the name of a zero-datum section the import created. Both appear on the receipt now, and a receipt carrying them opens the full summary rather than passing as a brief message ([#393](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/393))
- **Dropping a `.stalx` on the window did nothing.** It now opens the same way the file menu would, under the same checks, and waits rather than acting behind an open dialog
- **Notifications could cover the import wizard's own buttons** in a short window. They now stack above the wizard footer, and the stack shrinks its own capacity to match, so a full one cannot run off the top of the screen
- **An import into a brand-new project could fail to save it.** When the file needed you to place unmatched stations by hand first, finishing that step never wrote the result to disk, so a freshly imported survey could disappear when the app closed - and whether it did depended on unrelated imports earlier in the same session. Every import into a new project is written once it completes ([#411](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/411))
- **An import that brought in nothing, or lost part of the file on the way, used to report plain success.** Skipped shots, shot types the reader did not recognise and duplicate shot identities all went uncounted, and a file that produced no shots at all said nothing about it. Every import now accounts for what it did, including when the copy it saved could not be fully read back ([#411](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/411))
- **A failed or cancelled Ariane import left a half-made project behind, or moved you into one with no explanation.** The dialog now reports a failure before it navigates anywhere, and offers to throw away an empty project you never asked for. Progress shows in the dialog instead of appearing frozen, and the counts shown while previewing an import match what the next screen reports ([#411](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/411))
- **An import with nothing to warn about now finishes with a brief message instead of a dialog to dismiss.** A dialog still appears when the import converted units, raised a warning, or landed nothing ([#411](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/411))
- **The mNemo import wizard could finish without ever showing its receipt** - after placing stations by hand, or on the quick path through the last screen. The counts were right, they just never reached you. Both paths show the summary now ([#487](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/487))
- **Import polish**: the summary after a new-project import no longer says "1 sections"; the invitation to report a bug appears only when the failure could actually be one, not when the file is simply wrong; the import dialogs keep their buttons reachable when the body scrolls in a short window; and a project created by an import takes its name from the cave name in the file, when the file records one, rather than always from the filename ([#447](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/447))
- **Compass import preview counts are exact**, and the mNemo unemitted-reachable warning no longer repeats ([#437](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/437), [#535](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/535))


#### Windows

- **A Windows sweep: paths, save errors, byte-order marks, layout and map input.** Recent Projects no longer mangles drive letters or network paths. A save that fails to a too-long path, a missing folder or a disconnected drive says what actually happened, instead of showing a permissions dialog written for macOS. A `.srv`, `.dat`, `.dmp`, GPX or Ariane file that begins with a UTF-8 byte-order mark no longer silently loses its first line. Ctrl-drag and middle-drag panning no longer stop at the edge of the map, the data table's header no longer overlaps its own controls in a narrow window, and a UI scale of 3.0 no longer collapses the layout ([#429](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/429))
- **Zooming with a Windows trackpad ran at the speed meant for a mouse wheel** - sluggish, and able to change pace mid-gesture. Trackpad zoom is smooth now, the **Zoom speed**, **Pan speed** and **Invert scroll** settings under Map Controls do something for the first time, and zoom speed is even across its whole range rather than doing nothing over the top half of the slider ([#428](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/428))


#### Ariane export

- **The notice before an Ariane export no longer claims that sections will be discarded.** Every section is written; the notice now says only that the file will not be georeferenced, and points at **Set Zero Datum** on a station if that is not what you want ([#411](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/411))
- **Ariane opened an exported `.tml` to a blank map.** A section whose parent station was itself another section's merge station could be written with no parent at all, because parent links were resolved in section-storage order while the link tree is not ordered. Ariane reads a parentless non-entrance shot as a second entrance, abandons the loop solve, and draws nothing - the data table still filled, so the survey looked present but invisible. Only surveys that had both such a section *and* a loop closure were affected. Sections that genuinely stand alone are now written as their own entrance, the way Ariane stores them, and an export that would still leave a shot unrooted now fails instead of writing a file that cannot be drawn.
- **A survey with more than one entrance placed the extra ones at latitude 0, longitude 0.** Only the first entrance received the project's GPS anchor, so any other one landed roughly 9000 km away in the Atlantic and zooming to fit showed half a hemisphere. Every entrance now carries an anchor - its own section's datum where it has one, the project's otherwise - which is how Ariane writes them. Anchors stay put across repeated export/import cycles, and an export refused for unrooted sections lists a bounded, cleaned sample of them instead of an unbounded dump ([#378](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/378))
- **A survey that goes out to Ariane and comes back now keeps each shot's own identity** instead of every shot being issued a new one each round trip. It matters if you work on the same survey in both applications: a shot you split in Stalagmight keeps its identity in the half that used to lose it ([#423](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/423))
- **A shot comment no longer turns into a station comment on re-import.** A shot comment with no station comment beside it was written without the invisible marker that names its side, so a default re-import filed it on the station. Every shot comment now carries its marker ([#564](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/564))


#### Ariane import

- **Importing a `.tml` with "Keep comments on shots" destroyed station comments and left invisible characters in the text.** A `.tml` has one comment field per shot and no station comment at all, so Stalagmight writes both into that one field and marks the shot lines with an invisible character to tell them apart on the way back in. That decoding only ran when comments were being put back on stations: with **Keep comments on shots** ticked, the whole undecoded field was dropped into the shot comment - the station's text glued onto the shot's, with an invisible marker in front of it - and no station comment was created. Exporting again marked the already-marked text a second time, after which no later import could separate the two again and the station comment was gone for good. Both settings now decode the field; **Keep comments on shots** keeps every line on the shot, in the order it was written, with the markers removed. Markers are stripped from comment text as a `.tml` is read, so an import can no longer put one into a comment that later reaches a CSV or JSON export ([#483](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/483))
- **A `.tml` in imperial units could import at the wrong depth.** The starting depth of a branching section was left unconverted, which put it out by a factor of about 3.28, and the project's entrance elevation had the same problem. Both convert correctly now, and a file whose unit label the reader does not recognise warns instead of being read as metric ([#411](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/411))
- **Real `.tml`/`.tmlu` files could fail to import, or import with their text mangled**, over things Ariane itself writes: older text encodings, unexpected capitalisation in a few fields, and an unusually large file inside the archive. These import correctly now, with a warning wherever a character could not be read back exactly ([#411](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/411))
- **A depth-only survey exported to Ariane and read back complained that its pitch and depth disagreed, about data that says no such thing.** A level shot with no recorded pitch goes into the file as a wire value of exactly zero, and the reader took that back as a measured zero. It is read as "no pitch recorded" in exactly the cases where the warning would have been invented, and left alone where a recorded zero can be real ([#482](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/482))
- **Some branching `.tml` surveys were broken into more disconnected pieces than they needed to be.** The reader now follows the longest branch through a junction instead of stopping there, and rejoins side chains to the main line where that is unambiguous, so the imported shape is closer to how the cave was surveyed. Some fragmentation remains ([#412](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/412))
- **A `.tml`'s per-shot trip metadata now cuts sections instead of collapsing them** ([#427](https://github.com/Stalagmight/Stalagmight/issues/427))


## [0.2.0] - 2026-06-02

### Added
- **SVG/PNG/PDF export rewritten as a black-on-white stick map** - exports now draw only non-reference shots in one of two stroke widths, chosen per segment by a user-controlled branching threshold (the export dialog's "Line-weight threshold" slider). Zero-datum stations render as filled 5-pointed stars, and trunk (thick) segments are annotated with `p. Penetration distance is unified on a single `Project::compute_penetration_distances` Dijkstra metric shared by the Survey overview stat and the map labels.
- **Collaboration mode (Ariane)** - opt-in, per-project mode that keeps a sibling Ariane `.tml` file next to the `.stalx`. Every save writes both files (with native `<UUID>`s embedded for Ariane 26+); when a collaborator edits the `.tml` in Ariane and you reopen the project, Stalagmight detects the external edit and shows a **Collaboration Review** dialog previewing added/edited/removed shots, new sections, and section-metadata changes before merging them in as a single undoable operation. Shots are matched by UUID → Ariane integer ID → geometry fingerprint → position, so reconciliation survives Ariane's ID renumbering. Stalagmight-only data (loop closures, station merges, section/station offsets, dual azimuth, markers, temperature) is preserved on surviving shots. Enable it per project from the open project's **Project details** panel, or from the **recent-projects details panel** in the global list/map (which toggles a recent project without opening it); opening a project that has a companion `.tml` while collaboration is off warns that the file won't be kept in sync. (`.stalx` SQLite schema bumped to v5; older projects load unchanged with collaboration off)
- **Station comments survive Ariane round-trips** - station comments are now folded into the `.tml` Comment field of the shot whose `to` station matches, using an invisible zero-width-space (`U+200B`) prefix to distinguish shot vs station lines. Reconcile splits them back out, and an Ariane user's bare (unmarked) edits route to the station - matching how Ariane visually centers Comment text on the station. The fresh-import dialog's *"comments on stations"* option now applies the same split (default on).
